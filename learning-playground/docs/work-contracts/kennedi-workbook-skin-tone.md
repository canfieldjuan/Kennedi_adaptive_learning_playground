  illustrations."

## Before Code

### Root Cause

- Nothing in Kennedi's character spec names her skin tone. It is missing
  from the design system's canonical character section, from the face and
  hair lock's trait terms (`<FACE>`, `<HEAD>` in
  `workbook/docs/art/asset-provenance.md`), and from every prompt.
- FLUX fills the gap with its default. The one color image of her, the
  cover `design-source/scenes/cover-concept-c-final.png`, draws her
  light-skinned (`#FDC89A`) with bright pink cheeks. Page 1 and its proof
  page use it.
- The interior pages are Mode B line art. Her skin there is an uncolored
  outline for the child to color in, so they show no skin tone and are not
  wrong.
- The cover can't be fixed by re-rendering it. Its PNG embeds its graph
  (seed `1503776796`, 32 steps), so it is reproducible. But adding her skin
  to that prompt changes the conditioning, and the same seed then draws a
  different picture: the composition the operator approved would not
  survive. Only recoloring the existing image keeps it. (Its provenance
  entry says "Seed: new generation, not recorded"; that is wrong, and this
  slice corrects it.)

### Must Not Change

  prompts. `<FACE>` and `<HEAD>` stay as they are, and `<SKIN>` is for
  color prompts only: skin wording in a no-color prompt risks gray skin
  shading in print art.
- The original cover file, which is the tool's input.
- The cover's composition. Only skin, its shading and the blush may change.
- `workbook/src/styles/tokens.css`: print tokens, grayscale by design.
- Pages 2-6 and their `dist/` output.
- The recipe tool and the locked folders. The cover lives in `scenes/`,
  which is not a locked root.

## Behaviour

- `recolor-kennedi-skin.py SPEC.json` reads the source PNG and the palette
  that the spec names, and writes the output PNG it names.
  - **Mask:**
    - flat skin, by the spec's gates;
    - skin shading connected to it without crossing an outline, kept only
      where a region is at least half flat skin;
    - a 1 px edge into the outline;
    - nothing below the spec's ground row.
  - **Skin and shading:** the palette skin times (pixel luminance / source
    skin luminance). Shadows come out darker golden brown, not salmon.
  - **Cheeks:** the source blush (hue at most 14 degrees, in full light)
    and its darker rim are flattened to the skin, then blended toward the
    palette blush by a weight softened over 4 px, at the palette's
    strength.
  - Every other pixel is byte-identical to the source.
- `--check` recomputes the output and compares its pixels with the
  committed file. Any difference exits 1. Pixels, not bytes, because PNG
  encoders on different machines may write the same pixels differently.
- **Invariants:** the output is deterministic; pixels outside the mask are
  unchanged; the output's size and mode equal the source's.
- **Failure cases**, each exiting with a message before writing anything:
  - a missing source, palette or spec;
  - a spec without a required key;
  - an output path equal to the source.

## Settling Evidence (planned)

- `--check` passes on the committed cover, and fails once a palette value is
  changed.
- A pixel diff of source against output shows every changed pixel inside the
  mask, with the count reported, and nothing changed below the ground row or
  outside Kennedi.
- A before/after of page 1 goes to the operator. The face and arm close-ups
  match the approved preview.
- `npm run all` passes locally (after `npm ci` in this worktree), and the
  workbook gate is green.

## Contract Amendments

- **Root cause corrected before any code** (2026-10-04). The first version
  quoted the provenance entry's "seed not recorded" as the reason the cover
  can't be re-rendered. Reading the PNG showed its seed is embedded. The
  reason is now the true one: a changed prompt redraws the image. Scope
  gains one line, already inside the provenance item: the cover's entry
  records its seed.
- **The tool needs only numpy and Pillow.** The workbook gate's Python has
  no scipy. So the 4-connected region labelling and the cross-shaped
  dilation the preview took from `scipy.ndimage` are written out in the
  tool. Its output must equal the approved preview's pixels.
